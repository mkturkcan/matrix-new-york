Matrix New York

Street View initial observations and vehicle control for interactive video world models.

Mehmet Kerem Turkcan
Code Rollouts Release Matrix-Game-3.0
Matrix-Game-3.0 rollout from one Street View frame · FDR Drive · first 12 s of 24 s, 2× playback
Abstract

We drive a pretrained action-conditioned video world model through New York City starting from street-level imagery. For a query location we retrieve the nearest Google Street View panorama, reproject it along the capture heading, remove near-field vehicles, and insert a 3D vehicle rendered under a chase camera. A kinematic bicycle model converts throttle and steering into Matrix-Game-3.0 keyboard and camera-yaw actions. Because the model is first-person, we condition it on the vehicle-free frame and composite the ego vehicle from renders indexed by the controller's yaw relative to the camera. The pipeline requires no fine-tuning and runs on a single GPU.

Method

Panoramas are retrieved through Street View coverage tiles, which list only captures from Google's own vehicles, and downloaded at zoom 4 (zoom 3 if tiles are missing). The view is an equirectangular-to-perspective remap with yaw equal to the capture heading, pitch −3°, and 90° horizontal FOV. Near-field vehicles are segmented with Mask2Former (Swin-L, Cityscapes classes car, truck, bus, train, motorcycle, bicycle). A component is removed if it lies in the ego lane ahead, touches the bottom of the frame, or overlaps the inserted vehicle; parked vehicles at the sides are kept. Masks are dilated by 18 px, black no-data regions of the panorama are added, and LaMa fills the holes. The inserted vehicle is a CC-BY MeshFleet mesh rendered with pyrender from about two car lengths behind, composited at 31% of the image width with a contact shadow and a global gain/tint match.

Vehicle model and action mapping

Inputs are throttle/brake \(u\in[-1,1]\) and steering \(s\in[-1,1]\) (low-pass, \(\tau=0.15\) s). A kinematic bicycle model with speed-dependent steering authority and a first-order chase camera integrates them:

\[\dot v = a^{+}u^{+} - a^{-}u^{-} - c\,v,\quad \delta=\frac{s\,\delta_{\max}}{1+v/v_{\mathrm{ref}}},\quad \dot\psi=\frac{v}{L}\tan\delta,\quad \dot\psi_c=\frac{\psi-\psi_c}{\tau_c}\]

Matrix-Game-3.0 receives W with duty cycle \(\min(v/10\,\mathrm{m/s},1)\) and camera yaw \(\Delta\psi_c/15^\circ\) per frame; sub-deadzone yaw (|·| < 0.02) is accumulated. Lane changes are steering doublets, so the vehicle's heading changes and is never translated sideways. The ego vehicle is drawn from 17 renders (−24° to +24°, pivot at the rear axle) at the relative yaw \(\psi-\psi_c\).

Analysis

Observations

The ego vehicle must not be in the conditioning frame

Matrix-Game-3.0 has no ego-vehicle prior. A vehicle composited into the conditioning frame is treated as static scene content: the camera drives into it and it smears across the lower image within about a second. Conditioning on the inpainted frame and compositing the vehicle afterwards removes the artifact.

Forward speed is fixed by the model

Matrix-Game-3.0 advances a roughly constant distance per frame while W is held. The action interface can slow it down (W duty cycle) but not speed it up: scaling the camera translation used for its Plücker-ray conditioning left the output essentially unchanged. We therefore set apparent speed through temporal subsampling: the controller runs at 34 Hz and every second generated frame is displayed.

Limitations

Single observationOnly the first frame is observed. Content entering the view later is sampled from the model's prior and does not match the real street.
Long-horizon degradationQuality is highest in roughly the first 15 s of generated time. Afterwards facades simplify into flat geometry, road texture streaks, and color shifts. Forward driving rarely revisits earlier viewpoints, so camera-aware memory does not counteract this.
2D ego vehicleThe composited vehicle is not occluded by, does not shadow, and does not interact with generated content.
TurnsLarge heading changes away from intersections steer the camera into facades. Turn routes are only meaningful when the first frame is at an intersection.
