Matrix New York

Driving through New York City in action-conditioned video world models

Mehmet Kerem Turkcan  ·  Code Releases

Overview

We build initial observations for pretrained interactive video world models from Google Street View panoramas of New York City, and drive the models with a kinematic vehicle controller. For a query location we retrieve the nearest panorama captured by a Google Street View car and reproject it to a 1280×704 perspective view along the capture heading. We then remove near-field vehicles (Mask2Former segmentation, LaMa inpainting) and insert a vehicle mesh from MeshFleet rendered under a chase camera. Driver inputs (throttle, steering) are integrated by a kinematic bicycle model and mapped to the action spaces of two pretrained models: the Matrix-Game-2.0 GTA-drive checkpoint, a third-person driving model trained on GTA V, and Matrix-Game-3.0, a first-person model. No model is fine-tuned.

  • Matrix-Game-2.0 GTA-drive carries the inserted vehicle forward and produces non-holonomic motion: steering changes the vehicle's heading rather than translating it sideways, at speeds comparable to the source game. The scene, and to a lesser degree the vehicle, drift toward the GTA V appearance distribution after roughly 8–12 s, and the model reproduces the game's minimap in the lower-left corner.
  • Matrix-Game-3.0 keeps the photographic appearance of the initial frame for longer, at 704×1280, but has no notion of an ego vehicle. A vehicle in the conditioning frame is treated as static scene content and passed through within ~1 s. We therefore condition it on the vehicle-free frame and draw the vehicle from renders indexed by the controller's yaw relative to the camera. Its forward speed is fixed by the model and cannot be raised through the action interface (see Limitations).
  • Each clip runs on a single RTX PRO 6000 (Blackwell). Measured throughput after model load is ≈18 frames/s at 352×640 for Matrix-Game-2.0 GTA-drive and ≈3.6 frames/s at 704×1280 for Matrix-Game-3.0 (bf16, no int8, synchronous VAE).

Method

Initial observation

Example: TIMES SQUARE, panorama 2024-08, (40.7580, -73.9856).

  • Retrieval. The single-image search endpoint that streetlevel.streetview.find_panorama relies on has been decommissioned. We query Street View coverage tiles instead; they list only panoramas captured by Google's own vehicles. We fetch metadata by panorama id, prefer panoramas on the named road (e.g. FDR Drive over its service road), and download at zoom 4, falling back to zoom 3 when tiles are missing.
  • View. Equirectangular-to-perspective remap, yaw = capture heading (or a user-specified heading), pitch −3°, horizontal FOV 90°, output 1280×704.
  • Near-field vehicle removal. Mask2Former (Swin-L, Cityscapes) semantic classes {car, truck, bus, train, motorcycle, bicycle}. A connected component is removed if it is in the ego lane ahead, touches the bottom of the frame, is large or tall, or overlaps the footprint of the inserted vehicle. Parked vehicles at the image sides are kept. Masks are dilated by 18 px and extended downward to cover shadows; holes are filled with LaMa (big-lama). Black no-data regions of the panorama are added to the mask.
  • Vehicle insertion. CC-BY vehicle meshes from MeshFleet are rendered with pyrender (EGL) from about two car lengths behind and above the car, and composited at 31% of the image width with a two-layer blurred contact shadow and a global gain/tint match to the scene. For Matrix-Game-3.0 we additionally render 17 yaw angles (−24° to +24° in 3° steps) about the rear axle.

Vehicle model and action mapping

Inputs are throttle/brake \(u_t\in[-1,1]\) and steering \(s_t\in[-1,1]\) at the backend frame rate. The steering input is low-pass filtered (\(\tau=0.15\) s) and integrated with a kinematic bicycle model and a first-order chase camera:

\[ \dot v = a^{+}\max(u,0) + a^{-}\min(u,0) - c\,v,\qquad \delta = \frac{s\,\delta_{\max}}{1 + v/v_{\mathrm{ref}}},\qquad \dot\psi = \frac{v}{L}\tan\delta,\qquad \dot\psi_{c} = \frac{\psi-\psi_{c}}{\tau_c}, \]

with \(L=2.7\) m, \(\delta_{\max}=32^\circ\), \(v_{\mathrm{ref}}=6\) m/s, \(a^{+}=3\) m/s², \(a^{-}=7\) m/s², \(c=0.12\) s⁻¹, \(v\le 25\) m/s, \(\tau_c=0.35\) s. Lane changes are steering doublets (\(s=\pm0.7\) for 0.5 s each). Neither model has an analog throttle, so continuous commands are mapped to binary keys by pulse-width modulation.

The GTA-drive checkpoint (gta_distilled_model/gta_keyboard2dim.safetensors) was published in Skywork/Matrix-Game-2.0 and later removed from the main branch. We load it from revision f1729d9 and use the matching inference code from commit 756776d^ of SkyworkAI/Matrix-Game.

Limitations

  • Only the first frame is observed. Building layout, signage and traffic beyond it are sampled from the model's prior and do not correspond to the real street network.
  • GTA-drive outputs converge to GTA V appearance (geometry simplification, sky and water shading, vehicle identity) on the timescale of the clip. Shorter rollouts or periodic re-anchoring to Street View would be needed for geographic fidelity.
  • The Matrix-Game-3.0 ego vehicle is a 2D layer driven by the controller. It is not occluded by, does not cast shadows onto, and does not interact with generated content.
  • Large heading changes mid-block (e.g. 90° turns away from intersections) steer the camera into building facades and the rollout degrades. Turns are only meaningful when the initial frame is at an intersection.
  • Matrix-Game-3.0 moves a fixed distance per frame while W is held, so its maximum speed is set by the model, not the controller. Scaling the camera translation used for its Plücker-ray conditioning by 3× left the output essentially unchanged (mean Farnebäck flow 0.65 vs 0.66 px/frame at 320 px width). A lower W duty cycle does reduce speed (0.04 px/frame at 12% duty). The Matrix-Game-3.0 overlay therefore shows no speed readout.

Reproduction

python -m nycdrive.prepare_inputs                                # curated scenes
python -m nycdrive.prepare_inputs --random --seed 7              # random location
python -m nycdrive.prepare_inputs --lat 40.7497 --lon -73.9877 --name herald_square

python -m nycdrive.drive_gta --scene data/inputs/fdr_drive --route highway   # Matrix-Game-2.0 GTA-drive
python -m nycdrive.drive     --scene data/inputs/fdr_drive --route highway   # Matrix-Game-3.0
python -m nycdrive.drive     --scene data/inputs/fdr_drive --interactive     # step-wise keyboard control

Route presets (cruise, highway, downtown, slalom, turn_left, turn_right) are defined in nycdrive/vehicle.py; custom routes are JSON lists of [seconds, throttle, steer].
