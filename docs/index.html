Matrix New York

Street View initial observations and vehicle control for interactive video world models

Mehmet Kerem Turkcan

Code Release Videos

Overview

We drive pretrained action-conditioned video world models through New York City starting from street-level imagery. For a query location we retrieve the nearest Google Street View panorama, reproject it along the capture heading, remove near-field vehicles, and insert a 3D vehicle rendered under a chase camera. A kinematic bicycle model converts throttle and steering into each model's action space. First-person models are conditioned on the vehicle-free frame, and the ego vehicle is composited from renders indexed by the controller's yaw relative to the camera. No model is fine-tuned, and each rollout runs on a single GPU.

Method

Initial observation

Panoramas are retrieved through Street View coverage tiles, which list only captures from Google's own vehicles, and downloaded at zoom 4 (zoom 3 if tiles are missing). The view is an equirectangular-to-perspective remap with yaw equal to the capture heading, pitch −3°, and 90° horizontal field of view. Near-field vehicles are segmented with Mask2Former (Swin-L, Cityscapes classes car, truck, bus, train, motorcycle, bicycle). A component is removed if it lies in the ego lane ahead, touches the bottom of the frame, or overlaps the inserted vehicle; parked vehicles at the sides are kept. Masks are dilated by 18 px, black no-data regions of the panorama are added, and LaMa fills the holes. The inserted vehicle is a CC-BY MeshFleet mesh rendered with pyrender from about two car lengths behind, composited at 31% of the image width with a contact shadow and a global gain and tint match.

Vehicle model

Inputs are throttle and brake \(u\in[-1,1]\) and steering \(s\in[-1,1]\), low-pass filtered with \(\tau=0.15\) s. A kinematic bicycle model with speed-dependent steering authority and a first-order chase camera integrates them:

\[\dot v = a^{+}u^{+} - a^{-}u^{-} - c\,v,\quad \delta=\frac{s\,\delta_{\max}}{1+v/v_{\mathrm{ref}}},\quad \dot\psi=\frac{v}{L}\tan\delta,\quad \dot\psi_c=\frac{\psi-\psi_c}{\tau_c}\]

Lane changes are steering doublets, so the vehicle's heading changes and it is never translated sideways. For first-person models the ego vehicle is drawn from 17 renders (−24° to +24°, pivot at the rear axle) at the relative yaw \(\psi-\psi_c\).

Analysis

Ego vehicle in the conditioning frame

Matrix-Game-3.0 has no ego-vehicle prior. A vehicle composited into the conditioning frame is treated as static scene content: the camera drives into it and it smears across the lower image within about a second. Conditioning on the inpainted frame and compositing the vehicle afterwards removes the artifact.

Forward speed

Matrix-Game-3.0 advances a roughly constant distance per frame while W is held. Its action interface can slow it down through the W duty cycle but not speed it up: scaling the camera translation used for its Plücker-ray conditioning left the output essentially unchanged. We therefore set apparent speed through temporal subsampling. The controller runs at 34 Hz and every second generated frame is displayed.

Limitations

  • Only the first frame is observed. Content entering the view later is sampled from the model's prior and does not match the real street.
  • Quality is highest in roughly the first 15 s of generated time. Afterwards facades simplify into flat geometry, road texture streaks, and color shifts. Forward driving rarely revisits earlier viewpoints, so camera-aware memory does not counteract this.
  • The composited ego vehicle is a 2D layer. It is not occluded by, does not shadow, and does not interact with generated content.
  • Large heading changes away from intersections steer the camera into facades. Turn routes are only meaningful when the first frame is at an intersection.
